01 · Scope

A large dataset with a very practical question.

How can multiple fuel stations buy enough inventory to avoid stockouts, use supplier discounts more effectively, and avoid unnecessary capacity investment?

1.86M time-stamped fuel-level readings cleaned and modeled
2,831 usable purchase invoices analyzed
23 tanks across 8 anonymized stations
5 conceptual data sources integrated

The operating tension

Small, frequent orders keep cash outlay and inventory lower, but lose quantity discounts. Large orders unlock better pricing, but require storage headroom and raise the cost of holding stock.

03 · Findings

Three findings changed the decision.

The strongest opportunity was not simply buying more. It was matching order size to demand, capacity, and evidence quality.

04 · Decisions

Recommendations designed around constraints.

The result was a phased policy, not a single universal order quantity.

Replenishment

Use a rolling seven-day threshold.

Trigger reviews from demand by station and fuel type instead of applying one fixed reorder point to the whole network.

Order design

Consolidate only where headroom exists.

Test larger orders in lower-pressure groups first, while protecting Station A with tighter inventory monitoring.

Timing test

Pilot the weekday signal.

Collect more weekend observations and compare matched fuel types and order sizes before moving significant volume.

Measurement

Track the policy, not just the result.

Monitor stockout hours, order-size distribution, discount value, inventory coverage, and forecast error together.

46 full cycles to break even

Expansion scenario

Capacity expansion needs proof of repeatable use.

Under the project assumptions—a CAD 50,000 tank, 2% inflation, 40,000 liters of added capacity, and a 3-cent marginal discount—the five-year future cost is about CAD 55,204. The investment needs roughly 46 fully utilized incremental cycles, or 9.2 per year, before other costs.

05 · Judgment

Good analysis also explains what the data cannot prove.

Uneven weekday sample

The apparent Saturday advantage came from a very small sample, so it supports a controlled test rather than a network-wide change.

Excluded operating costs

Delivery cost, holding cost, lead-time uncertainty, maintenance, and financing were outside the source brief and should be included before implementation.

Two observation windows

The sensor files covered separate observation periods, so consumption was averaged within observed days rather than treated as one uninterrupted series.

Scenario, not promise

Discount and expansion figures are rule-based scenarios. They quantify the decision boundary; they do not guarantee realized savings.
